The 0–3m row covers birth; unlike heart rate, respiratory rate gets no separate birth row, because the curve declines smoothly with no early peak. The paper states a birth median of 44, against the 43 tabulated here. Respiratory data rests on 3,881 children against 143,346 for heart rate, so these bands are the softer of the two.

AAP columns are the 90th centile at the 5th height centile. A screening trigger only — the true cutoff runs up to 9 mmHg higher by height. Diagnosis needs auscultated readings at or above the 95th centile on three visits. Under 1 year the table does not apply. MAP is calculated as (SBP + 2 × DBP) / 3.

The last column is the PALS shock floor and is a systolic value, not a diastolic. From 13 years the adult categories take over: normal <120/<80, elevated 120–129 with DBP under 80, stage 1 at 130–139 or 80–89, stage 2 at 140 or 90. Because stage 1 is an or rule, 120/80 in a 14-year-old is stage 1 — the familiar “120/80 is normal” predates the 2017 guidelines.

Temperature and saturation

Rectal 36.6–38.0 °C, oral 35.5–37.5, axillary 36.5–37.5, tympanic 35.8–38.0. Age does not shift these — only the route. Fever is a rectal 38.0.

SpO2 94% and above is fine, below 90% act.
